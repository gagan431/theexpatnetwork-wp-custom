# THE EXPAT NETWORK — Social Media Strategy 2026

**Status:** Canonical working strategy
**Current phase:** 90-day authority, market-discovery and recruitment-validation cycle
**Primary language:** English
**Geographic positioning on social:** Europe-focused

---

## 1. Role of Social Media

THE EXPAT NETWORK uses social media to build:

1. authority;
2. qualified relationships;
3. market intelligence;
4. employer and candidate signals;
5. institutional credibility;
6. evidence for validating specialist international-talent corridors.

Social media is not primarily a follower-growth exercise.

The objective is to create qualified conversations and improve TEN's understanding of where international talent demand is genuinely actionable.

---

## 2. Platform Roles

### Founder LinkedIn

**AUTHORITY + RELATIONSHIPS + MARKET DISCOVERY**

Primary functions:

- publish original analysis;
- interpret labour-market evidence;
- test market hypotheses;
- build relationships with employers, recruiters and institutions;
- surface candidate and employer signals;
- develop founder authority around international talent, mobility and recruitment.

Locked founder headline:

**International Talent, Mobility & Recruitment | Europe | Founder, The Expat Network**

---

### TEN LinkedIn Page

**INSTITUTIONAL IDENTITY**

Primary functions:

- establish a credible institutional presence;
- publish sourced explainers;
- publish material TEN research observations;
- publish relevant product/platform milestones;
- publish verified programme information where appropriate;
- publish genuine cooperation announcements only when documented.

The TEN Page should not duplicate every founder post.

It is a credibility layer, not the primary organic distribution engine.

---

### Instagram

**DISCOVERY + HUMAN TRUST**

Primary functions:

- make complex labour-market insights easier to discover;
- humanise the founder and TEN;
- repurpose validated research into platform-native visual formats;
- generate saves, shares, profile visits and pathway/tool discovery.

Preferred formats:

- Reels;
- carousels;
- Stories;
- founder-led short video.

Do not compress regulated or high-stakes subjects into definitive short-form advice.

---

## 3. Geographic Positioning

Social positioning is **Europe-focused**.

Germany remains an important operational and product market.

India-Europe remains a priority corridor lens.

Do not collapse TEN's social identity into Germany-only positioning.

Do not imply that TEN already operates equally across every European market.

Expansion into specific countries, professions or corridors must be evidence-led.

---

## 4. Recruitment Positioning

Recruitment may be developed as:

**human-led specialist recruitment around validated talent corridors**

TEN is not positioning itself as:

- a generic recruitment agency;
- a job board;
- an ATS;
- a mass CV database;
- an AI-matching platform;
- an auto-apply service;
- a generic Europe-wide candidate marketplace.

Recruitment activity should emerge from validated evidence of:

1. candidate supply;
2. employer demand;
3. employer openness to international hiring;
4. manageable mobility and hiring friction;
5. a sufficiently narrow profession × geography corridor.

---

## 5. Core Content Thesis

The flagship content thesis is:

> **A skills shortage on paper does not always mean a real opportunity.**

TEN should repeatedly examine the difference between:

- official or reported shortage;
- actual vacancy demand;
- employer willingness to hire internationally;
- language requirements;
- qualification recognition;
- salary viability;
- work-authorisation friction;
- relocation feasibility.

Broad statements such as:

> Europe needs engineers.

should be avoided unless supported and sufficiently qualified.

Prefer narrow questions such as:

> Which engineering profiles are realistically recruitable internationally in which European markets, and what prevents hiring?

---

## 6. 90-Day Strategic Objective

The current 90-day objective is:

> **Establish The Expat Network as a credible evidence-led voice on European international talent mobility, while using content to discover and validate the specific profession × country × employer corridors where international recruitment demand is genuinely actionable.**

The three primary strategic functions are:

### Authority

Develop a recognisable evidence-led point of view around international hiring and mobility.

### Market Discovery

Use content and conversations to discover recurring candidate, employer and institutional signals.

### Recruitment Validation

Identify narrow international-talent corridors where specialist human-led recruitment may genuinely solve an employer problem.

## 7. Audience Hierarchy

### Tier 1 — Experienced International / Non-EU Professionals

People with meaningful professional experience exploring careers in Europe.

Core question:

> Is there actually a realistic European market for someone like me?

Important signals include:

- profession;
- experience;
- current country;
- target country/region;
- language level;
- recognition status;
- salary expectations;
- relocation readiness;
- work-authorisation situation.

Candidate engagement is also a source of market intelligence.

TEN must not create unrealistic expectations of employment.

---

### Tier 2 — Employers, Recruiters and Hiring Managers

Organisations and professionals dealing with hard-to-fill roles.

Core question:

> When does international recruitment genuinely solve a hiring problem?

Important signals include:

- recurring vacancies;
- local hiring difficulty;
- employer openness to international candidates;
- language flexibility;
- qualification constraints;
- salary;
- hiring urgency;
- relocation willingness;
- international onboarding friction.

This audience is central to validating whether a talent corridor has commercial recruitment potential.

---

### Tier 3 — Institutions and Labour-Mobility Ecosystem Organisations

Includes, where relevant:

- labour-market bodies;
- chambers;
- universities;
- mobility programmes;
- economic-development organisations;
- international skills initiatives;
- recognition ecosystem organisations.

Core requirement:

TEN must demonstrate evidence quality, source discipline and realistic interpretation.

Never imply institutional endorsement or partnership without documentation.

### Tier 4 — Relevant Specialists and Future Partners

## 10. Research Evidence Taxonomy

For internal content production, factual claims must be classified as:

### Primary-verified

Directly supported by a current official or primary source.

### Secondary-supported

Supported by credible secondary research but not yet independently confirmed through the strongest applicable primary source.

### Not-yet-primary-verified

Potentially useful evidence or hypothesis that requires primary verification before publication as fact.

### Unsupported

Not suitable for publication as a factual claim.

High-volatility or regulated claims require current official verification before publication.

## 11. Research-to-Content Workflow

Use:

**Signal
→ research
→ verification
→ insight
→ content
→ audience response
→ conversation
→ market evidence
→ updated hypothesis**

Public content should generally follow:

**Evidence
→ Meaning
→ Practical Action
→ Source
→ Relevant CTA**

AI tools may accelerate research synthesis and drafting but are not sources of truth.

---

## 12. Signal Quality

Do not treat one comment, message or anecdote as market validation.

Use the following progression:

### Anecdote

One isolated observation.

### Directional Signal

Multiple related observations or a credible external indication.

### Repeated Signal

The same issue appears repeatedly across independent conversations, employers, candidates or evidence sources.

### Validated Pattern

There is sufficient evidence from multiple independent sources and/or real market behaviour to justify operational action.

Recruitment investment should favour repeated signals and validated patterns.

## 15. CTA Framework

Not every post requires a CTA.

Valid CTA classes include:

- no CTA;
- thoughtful discussion;
- research question;
- follow / connect where justified;
- direct conversation;
- TEN `.org` pathway;
- TEN `.de` tool;
- official source;
- employer enquiry;
- candidate interest;
- institutional conversation;
- recruitment validation.

Avoid:

- "Agree?";
- forced "What do you think?";
- generic "DM me";
- fake scarcity;
- artificial urgency;
- engagement bait.

The CTA must follow legitimate user need and the content's strategic purpose.

---

## 16. Trust and Compliance Guardrails

Never publish:

- unsupported labour-shortage statistics;
- claims that shortage automatically means international opportunity;
- guaranteed jobs;
- guaranteed visas;
- guaranteed recognition outcomes;
- definitive immigration eligibility determinations by TEN;
- fabricated partnerships;
- fake employer relationships;
- invented testimonials;
- fake candidate success stories;
- artificial trust metrics.

For regulated or rapidly changing matters:

1. verify with current primary/official sources;
2. distinguish explanation from official determination;
3. use exact dates where relevant;
4. include source references;
5. record verification dates where appropriate.

---

## 17. Commercial Integrity

Commercialisation must sit behind legitimate user need.

Recruitment, referral or specialist relationships must not distort neutral labour-market analysis.

Do not recommend a provider because of commission.

Before referring users:

- verify provider legitimacy;
- disclose commercial relationships clearly;
- obtain consent before sharing personal information;
- review providers periodically.

## 19. Market-Discovery Loop

Social media should operate as a public market-research loop:

**Research
→ content
→ audience signal
→ conversations
→ stronger evidence
→ corridor hypothesis
→ validation
→ possible recruitment/institutional opportunity**

Important research questions include:

- Which professions repeatedly surface?
- Which countries/regions show genuine employer openness?
- Where is language the main blocker?
- Where is recognition the main blocker?
- Where does salary destroy viability?
- Where does relocation create disproportionate friction?
- Which India-Europe corridors deserve deeper validation?
- Which employer groups show genuine international-hiring interest?

---

## 20. Recruitment Validation Rules

A labour shortage alone is insufficient.

Before treating a corridor as recruitment-worthy, examine:

1. credible labour demand;
2. recurring vacancies;
3. employer willingness to hire internationally;
4. suitable candidate supply;
5. language requirements;
6. recognition requirements;
7. salary economics;
8. work-authorisation feasibility;
9. relocation feasibility;
10. evidence of employer or recruiter interest.

Do not build a mass candidate database as part of this process.
