# THE EXPAT NETWORK — Lead Magnet & Pipeline Principle

**Status:** Canonical guidance
**Effective:** 17 August 2026
**Applies to:** Founder LinkedIn, TEN website forms, `.org` orchestration, `.de` tools, talent-pipeline experiments and future recruitment-validation work.

---

## Core principle

> **Find one expensive uncertainty in the candidate journey and remove it before asking for their details.**

TEN should earn the right to request information from a candidate by first reducing a meaningful source of uncertainty, wasted effort, delay, cost or risk.

The lead magnet is therefore not the form, newsletter or PDF. The lead magnet is the useful resolution of a high-friction question.

Examples of expensive uncertainty include:

- whether a vacancy is realistically accessible to an international applicant;
- whether an employer has evidence of international hiring readiness;
- which country or market is realistic for a given profession and seniority;
- whether language or qualification recognition is likely to be a gating factor;
- whether the relevant work-authorisation route is potentially workable;
- whether salary and relocation economics make an opportunity viable;
- whether a programme, pathway or opportunity is live, planned or merely announced.

---

## Required sequence

Use this order wherever practical:

**Pain / uncertainty**
→ **research and verification**
→ **useful TEN explanation or tool**
→ **clear limitation / reality check**
→ **candidate next action**
→ **only then request the minimum useful details**

Do not reverse the sequence into:

**Give us your data first → maybe we will help later.**

---

## Lead-magnet standard

A TEN lead magnet should do at least one of the following:

1. prevent a low-probability or wasted application;
2. clarify a destination or profession decision;
3. identify a missing eligibility/readiness condition;
4. translate an official rule into a practical check;
5. identify a relevant official programme or specialist route;
6. reduce search time by consolidating verified, decision-relevant evidence;
7. expose an important unknown that the user should verify before proceeding.

Prefer interactive or decision-support utility over generic downloadable content.

Good examples may include:

- Vacancy Reality Check;
- International Employer Readiness Check;
- Europe Market-Fit Check;
- Work-Authorisation Reality Check;
- recognition/language readiness checks;
- verified programme/opportunity watchlists;
- profession × destination corridor checks.

Avoid generic lead magnets such as broad "10 tips" PDFs unless they solve a clearly validated problem better than a pathway, checklist or tool.

---

## Pipeline capture rule

Once TEN has delivered useful value, a candidate may be invited to share a private, minimal signal such as:

- profession / specialisation;
- relevant years of experience;
- current country;
- target destination(s);
- mobility stage;
- main blocker;
- timeframe / readiness where relevant;
- LinkedIn profile or contact detail where genuinely needed for follow-up.

Do not ask candidates to disclose these details publicly in LinkedIn comments when a private website form is more appropriate.

Do not request passports, residence permits, financial documents, health data or other sensitive documents for early-stage pipeline discovery.

Do not request CV uploads by default when the required signal can be obtained from a LinkedIn profile plus a small number of structured fields.

---

## Social-to-website funnel

Founder LinkedIn should normally perform this sequence for pipeline-oriented content:

**recognisable pain**
→ **verified insight**
→ **specific practical help**
→ **TEN website CTA**
→ **private qualification signal**
→ **appropriate routing**

The website form should clearly state what TEN will use the information for and should not imply a guaranteed job, visa, placement or official determination.

Candidate submissions should be routed to the appropriate next-step class:

A. TEN information on `.org`
B. TEN tool on `.de`
C. official source/programme
D. qualified specialist/partner
E. genuine recruitment conversation only where a validated opportunity or recruitment need exists

---

## Recruitment-validation boundary

TEN is not building a mass CV database, job marketplace, ATS or auto-matching platform.

The purpose of early pipeline capture is to identify repeated **profession × experience × source country × destination × blocker** patterns and validate whether a narrow talent corridor deserves deeper research or human-led recruitment work.

A labour shortage, candidate interest or high form volume alone does not validate a recruitment corridor.

Before treating a corridor as recruitment-worthy, continue to verify employer demand, international-hiring openness, language, recognition, salary, work-authorisation feasibility, relocation feasibility and genuine employer/recruiter interest.

---

## Product-development implication

Where a repeated expensive uncertainty appears across multiple candidates, TEN should first ask:

1. Can `.org` remove the uncertainty through better orchestration or official-source routing?
2. Would a narrow `.de` checker/tool remove it more efficiently?
3. Does an official system already solve it better?
4. Is a qualified specialist required instead?
5. Is the problem frequent and costly enough to justify building anything at all?

Only build when TEN is adding real orchestration or decision utility rather than duplicating the market.

---

## Trust rule

The value exchange must remain honest:

> **Help first. Ask second. Collect only what is necessary.**

Commercialisation, recruitment or referral opportunities sit behind legitimate user need and must never determine the recommendation presented to the user.
