# THE EXPAT NETWORK — Social Intelligence Operating System

**Status:** Canonical working operating model
**Primary use:** Weekly LinkedIn research, editorial planning, relationship activity and market-signal capture
**Active social channel for the current 30-day cycle:** LinkedIn only

---

## 1. Purpose

TEN uses social media as a research-and-relationship system, not merely a publishing calendar.

The operating loop is:

**Research → Verify → Publish → Participate → Converse → Capture Signals → Validate → Route**

The objective is:

**authority + qualified relationships + market intelligence + validated next actions**

---

## 2. Sunday — Research and Editorial Intelligence Day

Every Sunday, before planning the next week's LinkedIn posts, create one **Weekly Intelligence Pack**.

Do not scan every source indiscriminately.

Use question-driven retrieval.

The sequence is:

**Question → Geographic Scope → Primary Evidence → National Evidence → Original Research → Reality Radar → Market Signals → Content Angles → Source Log**

---

## 3. Step 1 — Select the Weekly Research Question

Classify the week before opening sources:

### EU-COMPARATIVE
A Europe-level or cross-border question.

Example:

> Which occupations show shortages across several European labour markets, and where does that still fail to translate into realistic international opportunity?

### CORRIDOR
A defined source-country → destination-country/region question.

Example:

> What does the evidence say about Indian engineering professionals accessing the Netherlands versus Germany?

### COUNTRY DEEP DIVE
A question about one national labour market.

Example:

> Is international healthcare recruitment currently realistic in Germany?

The geographic scope must be chosen before source selection to prevent Germany-only drift caused by source convenience.

---

## 4. Step 2 — Pull European Comparative Evidence First

For EU-COMPARATIVE questions, begin with the European Layer A source stack defined in `SOCIAL_CONTENT_SOURCE_STACK.md`.

Look specifically for:

- newly published reports;
- updated datasets;
- policy changes;
- occupation/country comparisons;
- labour-shortage and surplus signals;
- mobility barriers;
- migration-policy developments;
- recognition or skills forecasts.

Record for each material source:

- source;
- publication/update date;
- what changed;
- countries covered;
- occupations covered;
- claim supported;
- limitations;
- last verified date;
- potential content angle.

---

## 5. Step 3 — Pull National Evidence Only Where Needed

Use Layer B national sources only when:

- the research question is country-specific; or
- an EU-level finding needs national validation.

Do not default to Germany simply because German data is easier to access.

For multi-country questions, identify the relevant national authorities for each selected market.

---

## 6. Step 4 — Add Original Research

Use Layer C original research where official sources do not adequately answer the question.

Prefer:

- original datasets;
- peer-reviewed or academic work;
- reputable research institutes;
- employer surveys with disclosed methodology;
- sector studies with transparent sampling.

Prefer the original study over journalism describing the study.

---

## 7. Step 5 — Run the Reality Radar

Use Layer D sources to identify real concerns, emerging stories and questions worth testing.

The Local Germany may be used as the **Expat Reality Radar**.

Use:

**Article → pain point / claim → original or official source → verification → original TEN interpretation**

Do not copy, closely paraphrase or treat The Local as an authority for regulated or official decisions.

---

## 8. Step 6 — Review Market Signals

Review Layer E signals from the previous week:

- employer comments;
- recruiter conversations;
- candidate experiences;
- institutional comments;
- LinkedIn posts and comments;
- specialist observations.

Classify each signal as:

- Anecdote;
- Directional Signal;
- Repeated Signal;
- Validated Pattern.

Repeated signals should influence the next Weekly Intelligence Pack.

---

## 9. Weekly Intelligence Pack — Required Output

Each Sunday pack should remain concise.

Use this structure:

### Research question

One primary question.

### Geographic scope

EU-COMPARATIVE / CORRIDOR / COUNTRY DEEP DIVE.

### Primary evidence

Approximately 5–8 strongest applicable sources where available.

### Key verified findings

Approximately 3–5 findings.

### Contradictions / uncertainty

Important evidence that conflicts or remains unresolved.

### Candidate implications

What the evidence may mean for experienced international / non-EU professionals.

### Employer implications

What the evidence may mean for employers, recruiters or hiring managers.

### Open questions

Questions that should not yet be answered as facts.

### Market signals worth testing

Signals that can be explored through LinkedIn content or conversations.

### Potential LinkedIn angles

A small set of angles derived from the same research package.

### Claims not safe to publish yet

Claims that remain unsupported or insufficiently verified.

---

## 10. One Research Package → Multiple Content Angles

Do not run five independent research projects merely because five posts are planned.

The preferred model is:

**one weekly research package → multiple evidence-led angles**

Durable content lenses:

1. Market Reality;
2. Candidate Reality;
3. Employer Reality.

Content functions:

4. Market Discovery — designed to learn something;
5. Interpretation — designed to synthesise evidence and signals.

This reduces research duplication while maintaining editorial variety.

## 11. Current Week-1 LinkedIn Timing Scan

The first five-day test is a **Timing Scan**, not a controlled experiment.

Current slots:

- Monday — 08:30 CEST / 12:00 IST;
- Tuesday — 12:30 CEST / 16:00 IST;
- Wednesday — 15:00 CEST / 18:30 IST;
- Thursday — 17:30 CEST / 21:00 IST;
- Friday — 11:00 CEST / 14:30 IST.

The scan deliberately generates directional observations.

Do not declare a winning day or time after one observation per slot.

Every timing conclusion must be documented as:

- Observed result;
- Sample size;
- Possible confounders;
- Hypothesis;
- Next test.

Example:

> Thursday 17:30 generated the strongest signal in Week 1. This remains an unconfirmed hypothesis based on n=1 for that slot and requires replication.

## 12. Founder LinkedIn Commenting Strategy

Commenting is used for:

- relationship building;
- market research;
- expertise demonstration;
- audience shaping;
- surfacing employer, recruiter and institutional signals.

It is not an engagement-hacking routine.

### Initial cadence

Run two focused Founder commenting sessions per week:

- Tuesday — approximately 20–30 minutes;
- Thursday — approximately 20–30 minutes.

Aim for approximately 5–8 genuinely useful comments per session maximum.

Quality is more important than volume.

---

## 13. Founder Commenting Targets

### Bucket A — Institutions

Examples:

- ELA;
- EURES;
- European Commission;
- CEDEFOP;
- chambers;
- employment authorities;
- labour-market and migration researchers.

Objective:

**institutional understanding + authority adjacency**

### Bucket B — Employers

Prioritise employers connected to corridors TEN is actively researching.

Look for posts about:

- vacancies;
- hard-to-fill roles;
- workforce planning;
- international hiring;
- onboarding;
- language requirements;
- relocation.

Objective:

**understand hiring friction + demonstrate useful perspective**

### Bucket C — Recruiters / Hiring Specialists

Prioritise specialist recruiters in selected professions and markets.

Objective:

**surface real hiring friction that official datasets do not measure directly**

### Bucket D — Researchers / Practitioners

Prioritise people publishing serious work on:

- labour-market conditions;
- mobility;
- recognition;
- skills forecasts;
- international recruitment.

Objective:

**participate in the substantive professional conversation**

---

## 14. Comment Types

A strong Founder comment should usually do one of four things:

### Add evidence

Introduce a relevant verified fact or source.

### Add practical meaning

Explain why the issue matters to international candidates or employers.

### Ask a real research question

Ask something TEN genuinely wants to understand.

### Add a measured counterpoint

Introduce a limitation or distinction without becoming adversarial.

Avoid generic comments such as:

- Great post;
- Totally agree;
- Very insightful;
- Thanks for sharing.

Also avoid:

- aggressive corrections;
- hijacking someone else's post;
- unnecessary TEN links;
- pitching services;
- unsupported expertise claims;
- institutional name-dropping;
- partnership implication.

---

## 15. TEN LinkedIn Page Commenting

The TEN Page has a different role.

Use Page comments selectively for:

- institutional research;
- official programmes;
- relevant ecosystem developments;
- material labour-market publications.

Initial operating range:

**1–3 meaningful institutional comments per week**

The Page should feel institutional, not omnipresent.

---

## 16. Comments as Market Evidence

A comment or reply can become Layer E evidence.

Example:

If multiple unrelated employers independently state that language requirements are blocking international hiring, classify the pattern as a repeated employer signal.

Do not immediately publish it as a market-wide fact.

Instead:

**Signal → next research question → primary/source verification → stronger evidence or rejection**

This creates the operating loop:

**Research → Content → Comment → Conversation → Signal → Research**

## 18. Performance and Signal Capture

Prioritise:

### Primary strategic signals

- qualified comments;
- qualified DMs / conversations;
- employer, recruiter and institutional engagement;
- relevant profile actions where visible.

### Secondary signals

- out-of-network reach;
- saves;
- sends / reposts;
- relevant follower conversion.

### Context metrics

- impressions;
- reactions;
- raw engagement rate.

Do not let a high-impression but strategically irrelevant post overrule a smaller post that generates valuable market evidence or qualified relationships.

## 19. Minimal Data Model

Do not build a CRM or complex social analytics system for this workflow.

Maintain only the minimum data required to learn:

### CONTENT LOG

- date;
- platform/account;
- post URL;
- content lens/function;
- geographic scope;
- profession/sector;
- format;
- research package;
- source status;
- timing slot;
- key metrics;
- qualified responses;
- learning;
- decision: KEEP / MODIFY / TEST AGAIN / DROP.

### SIGNAL LOG

- date;
- signal source;
- signal type;
- audience type;
- profession;
- country;
- issue;
- evidence level;
- repeated?;
- follow-up required?;
- next research question.

Do not unnecessarily store sensitive personal information, CVs, private immigration details, telephone numbers or personal lead data in the research log.

## 20. Automation Principle

Automate **retrieval prompts, reminders, source checks and pack generation**, not editorial judgement.

The automation may:

- trigger the Sunday Weekly Intelligence Pack workflow;
- remind TEN to review the previous week's LinkedIn analytics and signals;
- search current European primary sources for relevant updates;
- create a draft Weekly Intelligence Pack;
- flag high-volatility claims for re-verification.

The automation must not:

- auto-publish LinkedIn posts;
- auto-comment on third-party posts;
- auto-DM prospects;
- promote unverified claims;
- convert one social signal into a validated market claim;
- create partnership implications;
- make regulated or official determinations.

Human review remains mandatory before publication or outreach.

---

## 21. Recommended Automation Architecture

Use a low-complexity three-part system:

### A. Sunday ChatGPT Task

A recurring Sunday task triggers the Weekly Intelligence Pack workflow.

The task instructs ChatGPT to:

1. read the current canonical TEN social strategy and source stack;
2. review the previous week's supplied performance and signal data where available;
3. identify the next high-value research question;
4. search the strongest applicable current European and national sources;
5. classify evidence;
6. produce the draft Weekly Intelligence Pack;
7. identify claims requiring human or source re-verification;
8. stop before writing or publishing final LinkedIn posts.

### B. Manual LinkedIn Input

The founder supplies:

- relevant LinkedIn comments or screenshots;
- notable employer/recruiter/institutional posts;
- native LinkedIn analytics;
- paid The Local material when relevant.

These inputs cannot be reliably or safely automated without unnecessary complexity and access risk.

### C. Human Editorial Gate

The founder and ChatGPT review the pack and explicitly choose:

- the week's geographic scope;
- the research thesis;
- the content angles;
- the source status of claims;
- what not to publish.

Only after this gate should content production begin.

---

## 22. Scope-Control Rule

If maintaining the intelligence system becomes more work than publishing and relationship building, simplify it.

Do not build:

- a custom crawler;
- a social-listening SaaS;
- a CRM integration;
- automated LinkedIn commenting;
- automated prospecting;
- a complex source database;
- a separate dashboard;

until a validated operational problem requires one.

The MVP operating principle is:

**one weekly question → strongest relevant evidence → one intelligence pack → multiple content angles → two relationship sessions → signal capture → next question.**
